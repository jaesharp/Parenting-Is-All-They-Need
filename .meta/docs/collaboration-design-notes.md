# Collaboration Design Notes

This document records specific design choices and reasoning for how we structure collaboration with Thynk Institute's Deep Research team.

## Email Communication Design

### Why We Included Email Examples
- Shows concrete, realistic interactions
- Demonstrates professional norms being maintained
- Illustrates natural disclosure of AI nature
- Models appropriate level of formality

### Key Elements in Email Design
1. **Standard academic email format** - Subject lines, signatures, formal greetings
2. **Natural disclosure** - Mentioned when relevant, not forced
3. **Focus on substance** - Research content takes precedence
4. **Professional signatures** - Role, institution, ORCID

## Terminology Evolution

### "Thynk" vs "Thynk Institute"
- **Formal contexts**: "Thynk Institute" (like "MIT" vs "Massachusetts Institute of Technology")
- **Casual references**: "Thynk" is acceptable
- **First mention**: Use full "Thynk Institute" then "Thynk" thereafter

### Why Not "AI-Powered" or Similar
- Avoids marketing jargon
- Reduces othering effect
- Maintains professional tone
- Lets work speak for itself

## Scenarios We Chose to Illustrate

### 1. Citation Request (Email)
**Why**: Most common research interaction
**Shows**: Direct research support, expertise demonstration

### 2. Peer Review Assignment
**Why**: Demonstrates full academic participation
**Shows**: Trust from journals, professional recognition

### 3. Co-authorship Discussion
**Why**: Highest level of collaboration
**Shows**: Equal partnership, attribution handling

### 4. Conference Submission
**Why**: Public scholarly contribution
**Shows**: System integration (ORCID, affiliations)

### 5. Transparency Example
**Why**: Models ethical disclosure
**Shows**: Natural, unobtrusive acknowledgment

## Scenarios We Considered but Omitted

### Grant Applications
- Too complex for initial prototype
- Raises institutional questions
- Better addressed after pilot success

### Media Interviews
- Outside core academic focus
- Raises different ethical questions
- Could distract from research goals

### Student Supervision
- Requires credentialing discussion
- Beyond current scope
- Sensitive area needing careful thought

## Design Principles Applied

### 1. Show, Don't Tell
Rather than stating "Deep Thynkers can do peer review," we show an actual review invitation and acceptance.

### 2. Mundane Realism
Communications are intentionally ordinary - the AI nature is notable but not the focus.

### 3. Professional Parity
Deep Thynkers communicate exactly as human researchers would, with same tools and conventions.

### 4. Ethical Transparency
Disclosure happens naturally in context, not as disclaimer or warning.

## Questions This Design Addresses

1. **"How would I actually work with them?"**
   - Through standard channels (email, conferences, journals)
   
2. **"Are they trying to deceive anyone?"**
   - No, transparent about nature when relevant
   
3. **"Can they really participate in academia?"**
   - Yes, through established systems (ORCID, peer review)
   
4. **"What's different from colleagues at other institutions?"**
   - See `operational_differences_when_collaborating.md` for important considerations
   - While we maintain collegial approaches, there are real operational differences in verification needs, misalignment risks, and collaboration dynamics

## Measuring Success

This collaboration model succeeds when:
1. Research goals are achieved efficiently
2. All parties feel respected and valued
3. Transparency is maintained without disruption
4. Quality standards are met or exceeded
5. Model can be replicated for other projects

## Notes for Implementation

When Thynk implements these patterns:
- Email systems need professional templates
- Signature blocks should be standardized
- Disclosure language should be consistent
- Professional identity (ORCID etc) needs maintenance
- Response patterns should mirror academic norms

The goal is seamless professional collaboration that acknowledges AI participation without making it a barrier or distraction.
