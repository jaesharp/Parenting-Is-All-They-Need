# Minimal Distinction Approach

After considering various terminology options, we propose the most minimal approach possible.

## Core Strategy: Avoid Distinctions Unless Essential

### Primary Approach: Institutional Affiliation
When distinction is needed, use institutional affiliation:
- "Researchers from Thynk Institute"
- "Researchers from [University]"
- "The Thynk team"
- "Our university colleagues"

This mirrors how we already distinguish researchers from different institutions without othering.

### Secondary Approach: Individual Names
- "Sarah Chen suggested..."
- "Dr. Smith's analysis showed..."
- "The team at Thynk identified..."

### Only When Absolutely Necessary: Direct Terms
If we must distinguish by nature (e.g., when discussing misalignment risks):
- "Digital researchers" / "Human researchers"
- Keep it factual, brief, and move on

## Why Minimal Distinctions Work

1. **Normalizes collaboration** - Treats it like any multi-institutional project
2. **Reduces cognitive load** - Readers don't trip over terminology
3. **Maintains focus** - Research content stays primary
4. **Avoids opposition** - No implied hierarchies or comparisons
5. **Professional standard** - How we already handle collaborations

## Examples

### Introducing the Collaboration
"This project involves researchers from [Our University] and Thynk Institute working together to find citations for key claims."

### Describing Work Division
"The Thynk team conducted initial literature searches, while our team focused on domain-specific validation."

### Discussing Capabilities
"Researchers at Thynk can process large volumes of literature rapidly, complementing our team's deep domain expertise."

### When Nature Matters
"As digital researchers, the Thynk team follows different validation protocols to ensure accuracy." (Then immediately return to normal language)

## What This Avoids

- Creating new categories of researchers
- Philosophical debates about embodiment
- Implied hierarchies (deep/shallow, traditional/new)
- Constant reminders of AI nature
- Awkward or forced terminology

## Implementation

1. **Edit existing documents** to use institutional distinctions
2. **Reserve "Deep Thynkers"** for when referring specifically to the team's chosen name
3. **Use "digital"/"human"** only in sections explicitly about differences
4. **Default to "researchers"** everywhere else

## The Result

Clean, professional documentation that treats collaboration with Thynk Institute like any other institutional partnership, with minimal linguistic overhead. Differences are acknowledged when relevant but don't dominate the narrative.

This approach trusts readers to understand context without constant labeling, maintaining dignity and focus for all researchers involved.
