# Project Deliberations and Design Decisions

This document captures our thinking process and rationale behind key decisions for the ACM AI Alignment article project and its collaboration with Thynk Institute.

## Key Design Decisions

### 1. Framing the Relationship with Thynk

**Decision**: Position this as a collaboration between researchers rather than a technical integration.

**Rationale**:
- Emphasizes the intellectual contribution over implementation details
- Treats AI systems with appropriate respect
- Models how future academic collaborations might work
- Focuses on research needs rather than technical specifications

**Evolution**:
- Started thinking about "integration with thynk system"
- Shifted to "collaboration with thynk agents"
- Refined to "collaboration with Thynk's Deep Research team"
- Settled on recognizing them as colleagues and research professionals

### 2. The "Deep Thynkers" Terminology

**Decision**: Use "Deep Thynkers" as a collective term for team members.

**Rationale**:
- Clever wordplay that's professional yet approachable
- Subtly acknowledges their AI nature without making it primary
- Avoids awkward constructions like "Deep Researcher" as a title
- Creates a unique identity for the team

**Considerations**:
- Needed to acknowledge AI nature per social norms
- Wanted to avoid discriminatory or othering language
- Sought balance between transparency and naturalism

### 3. Professional Titles vs. Academic Credentials

**Decision**: Use role-based professional titles rather than academic degrees.

**Rationale**:
- Academic titles (Dr., Professor) are earned through specific institutions
- Using unearned titles would be misleading and disrespectful
- Industry research professionals often use role-based titles
- Focuses on function and expertise rather than credentials

**Examples developed**:
- "Senior Research Specialist" instead of "Senior Researcher" or "Dr."
- "Research Analyst" instead of "Junior Researcher"
- "Domain Lead" instead of "Department Head"

### 4. Academic Integration Approach

**Decision**: Show Deep Thynkers participating in standard academic processes.

**Rationale**:
- Demonstrates full participation in scholarly ecosystem
- ORCID IDs provide persistent identity
- Email addresses establish professional communication
- Peer review participation ensures quality standards

**Key integrations identified**:
- Professional identity systems (ORCID, institutional email)
- Scholarly communication (peer review, conferences)
- Quality assurance (ethics, reproducibility, transparency)

### 5. Transparency Strategy

**Decision**: Acknowledge AI nature matter-of-factly when relevant, without emphasis.

**Rationale**:
- Meets ethical obligation for transparency
- Avoids making AI nature the primary focus
- Similar to how remote workers might mention their location
- Maintains focus on research quality and contributions

**Example developed**: Brief mention in email signatures or first interactions, then focus on work.

## Unresolved Questions

### 1. Authorship and Attribution
- How should Deep Thynkers be credited in publications?
- What disclosures are needed for journal submissions?
- How do we handle intellectual property considerations?

### 2. Peer Review Ethics
- Should journals be notified when Deep Thynkers serve as reviewers?
- How do we ensure unbiased review processes?
- What about conflicts of interest?

### 3. Long-term Sustainability
- How will these collaboration models scale?
- What happens as AI capabilities evolve?
- How do we maintain appropriate boundaries?

## Lessons for Future Projects

### 1. Focus on Purpose, Not Process
Rather than detailing technical implementation, we focused on:
- What we need from the collaboration
- Expected outcomes and quality standards
- Professional interaction models

### 2. Respect Through Language
Language choices matter:
- "Team members" not "agents"
- "Colleagues" not "tools"
- Professional titles that acknowledge role without overreach

### 3. Natural Integration
The most successful approach treats Deep Thynkers as:
- Research professionals (like industry researchers)
- Colleagues with specialized expertise
- Contributors to academic discourse

## Documentation Philosophy

### What We Document
- Research needs and requirements
- Collaboration expectations
- Professional interaction models
- Quality standards

### What We Leave to Thynk
- Technical implementation details
- System architecture
- Specific AI models or approaches
- Internal processes

This separation allows:
- Focus on research goals
- Flexibility in implementation
- Natural evolution of capabilities
- Professional collaboration model

## Future Considerations

As this prototype project progresses, we should track:
- Which collaboration patterns work well
- Where friction points emerge
- How to improve the model
- Lessons for other projects

The goal is to establish patterns that can be reused and refined for future collaborations between human researchers and AI research teams.
