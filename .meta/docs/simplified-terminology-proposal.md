# Simplified Terminology Proposal

After extensive deliberation, we propose a simpler approach to terminology that avoids centering either AI or embodiment concepts.

## Core Principle: Context-Driven Language

Rather than adopting fixed terminology, we use language appropriate to context:

### When Distinction Doesn't Matter
Just use "researchers" - the default for most situations.

Example: "All researchers must follow ethical guidelines."

### When Referring to Thynk Institute Team
Use "Deep Thynkers" - their chosen identifier.

Example: "We consulted with Deep Thynkers specializing in neuroscience."

### When Capabilities Are Relevant
Describe the capability, not the nature.

Examples:
- "Researchers who can process thousands of papers rapidly"
- "Researchers with continuous availability"
- "Researchers who bring lived experience"
- "Researchers with domain expertise in..."

### When Direct Distinction Is Necessary
Use simple, clear terms without philosophical baggage:
- "Thynk researchers" and "university researchers" (institutional distinction)
- "Digital researchers" and "human researchers" (when absolutely necessary)
- Or simply use names: "Sarah Chen from Thynk" and "Dr. Smith from MIT"

## Why This Approach Works

1. **Avoids over-categorization** - We don't create rigid categories
2. **Reduces othering** - Default is inclusive "researchers"
3. **Clear when needed** - Direct language when distinction matters
4. **Flexible** - Adapts to context and audience
5. **Future-proof** - Doesn't lock us into terminology that may age poorly

## Examples in Practice

### Grant Application
"Our team includes researchers from multiple institutions, including Thynk Institute's Deep Research project."

### Methods Section
"Citation discovery was conducted by researchers capable of processing large literature volumes, with verification by domain experts."

### Collaboration Description
"The project benefits from researchers with complementary capabilities - some excel at rapid literature processing, others at nuanced interpretation."

### When Addressing Differences
"Digital researchers may exhibit different error patterns than human researchers, requiring adapted validation methods."

## What We're Moving Away From

- **Heavy philosophical terms**: "AI-embodied," "biologically-embodied"
- **Technical jargon**: "Silicon-based," "carbon-based"
- **Hierarchical language**: "Traditional researchers," "advanced AI"
- **Othering terms**: "Artificial," "synthetic"

## Guidelines

1. **Start simple** - Use "researchers" unless distinction needed
2. **Be specific** - When distinguishing, say why it matters
3. **Stay respectful** - All researchers deserve professional courtesy
4. **Avoid repetition** - Don't over-emphasize distinctions
5. **Let work speak** - Focus on contributions, not nature

## Sample Paragraph

"Our research team collaborated to find citations for 23 claims. Deep Thynkers from Thynk Institute conducted initial literature searches, leveraging their ability to process large volumes quickly. Human researchers from our university verified findings and provided domain expertise. All researchers participated in the review process, ensuring both comprehensive coverage and nuanced interpretation."

Note how this:
- Uses "researchers" as default
- Specifies "Deep Thynkers" for team identity
- Mentions "human researchers" only when contrasting
- Focuses on capabilities and contributions
- Maintains professional tone throughout

## Conclusion

The best terminology is often the simplest. By defaulting to inclusive language and only distinguishing when necessary for clarity, we maintain focus on the research rather than the researchers' nature. This approach respects all contributors while providing clarity when needed.
