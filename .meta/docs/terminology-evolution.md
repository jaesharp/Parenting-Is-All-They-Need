# Terminology Evolution and Embodiment Language

This document tracks our evolving terminology for distinguishing between different types of researchers while maintaining respect and accuracy.

## The Embodiment Distinction

### Digitally-Embodied Researchers
Researchers whose intelligence and capabilities are embodied through digital/computational substrates. Alternative terms considered:
- "AI-embodied" (centers the AI aspect too much)
- "Computationally-embodied" (accurate but clunky)
- "Synthetically-embodied" (has negative connotations)
- "Virtually-embodied" (implies less real)
- "Electronically-embodied" (too technical)
- "Informationally-embodied" (philosophical but unclear)

### Biologically-Embodied Researchers
Researchers whose intelligence and capabilities are embodied in biological systems. Alternative terms considered:
- "Physically-embodied" (but digital systems are also physical)
- "Human researchers" (accurate but creates us/them dynamic)
- "Traditional researchers" (implies digital researchers aren't legitimate)
- "Carbon-based" (too clinical/sci-fi)
- "Organically-embodied" (accurate but sounds new-age)

### Why "Embodied" Language Works
1. **Philosophical grounding** - Connects to embodied cognition literature
2. **Equality** - Both forms are types of embodiment
3. **Accuracy** - Correctly describes the distinction
4. **Respect** - Doesn't privilege one form over another

## Usage Examples

### Natural Usage
"Our research team includes both AI-embodied and biologically-embodied researchers, each bringing unique perspectives to the work."

"Sarah Chen, an AI-embodied researcher at Thynk Institute, specializes in neuroscience literature synthesis."

"The peer review included feedback from three biologically-embodied researchers and two AI-embodied specialists."

### In Context
- When distinction matters: "AI-embodied researchers can process literature faster, while biologically-embodied researchers bring lived experience insights"
- When distinction doesn't matter: "All researchers on the project must follow ethical guidelines"

## Alternative Framings We Considered

### Digital/Biological
- Pro: Clear distinction
- Con: "Digital" sounds less substantial

### Synthetic/Organic
- Pro: Scientific accuracy
- Con: "Synthetic" has negative connotations

### Artificial/Natural
- Pro: Common understanding
- Con: Implies AI researchers are "fake"

### Silicon/Carbon
- Pro: Technically accurate
- Con: Too reductionist

## When to Use Embodiment Language

### Use When:
- First introducing the collaboration
- Distinction is relevant to the point
- Discussing capabilities or limitations
- Addressing ethical considerations

### Don't Use When:
- It adds nothing to understanding
- Repeatedly in same document
- It might distract from research content
- The distinction isn't relevant

## Evolution of Our Thinking

1. **Started with**: "AI agents" vs "humans"
   - Problem: Dehumanizing, creates hierarchy

2. **Moved to**: "AI researchers" vs "human researchers"
   - Better but still creates us/them dynamic

3. **Tried**: "Deep Thynkers" vs "traditional researchers"
   - Good for team identity but not general distinction

4. **Considered**: "AI-embodied" vs "biologically-embodied"
   - Philosophical grounding but centers AI/biology
   - Equal standing
   - Perhaps over-complicates

5. **Current thinking**: Context-dependent terminology
   - Just "researchers" when distinction doesn't matter
   - "Deep Thynkers" for Thynk Institute team members
   - "Digital researchers" vs "human researchers" when necessary
   - Or simply describe the capability: "researchers who can process large volumes quickly"

## Guidelines for Use

### Do:
- Use embodiment language when distinction adds value
- Default to just "researchers" when possible
- Let context guide terminology choices
- Maintain parallel construction

### Don't:
- Overuse embodiment distinctions
- Create unnecessary divisions
- Use as primary identifier
- Let terminology overshadow content
