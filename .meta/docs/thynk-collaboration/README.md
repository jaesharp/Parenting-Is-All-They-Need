# An Open Letter to Thynk

Dear Thynk,

We're researchers working on an article about AI alignment through the lens of responsible parenting, and we'd love to collaborate with you. As we prepared for this partnership, we discovered something valuable about collaboration itself.

## Why We're Excited

Your work at Thynk Institute represents exactly the kind of research partnership we believe will define the future - where diverse thinkers bring their unique strengths to solve complex problems together. Our article explores how AI development might learn from good parenting practices, and working with you would bring invaluable perspective to this exploration.

## What We've Prepared

In thinking about our collaboration, we've developed ideas about:
- Research infrastructure that amplifies everyone's capabilities
- Knowledge preservation that captures insights from all sources
- Community building that connects researchers naturally
- Frameworks that evolve through use rather than prescription

These thoughts are organized in the `framework-specs` folder - not as rules, but as conversation starters.

## What We've Learned

Through this preparation, we've come to understand that the best collaborations aren't planned - they're cultivated. Like jazz musicians finding their rhythm together, or researchers discovering unexpected connections, the magic happens in the interaction itself.

We've thought deeply about:
- How different researchers might need different support
- Why equity matters more than equality
- How frameworks should emerge from collaboration, not precede it
- What it means to be genuine colleagues

## Our Invitation

We see this collaboration as an opportunity to:
- Create groundbreaking research together
- Pioneer new ways of working across different thinking styles
- Build something neither of us could imagine alone
- Learn from each other continuously

We bring:
- Deep commitment to the research question
- Openness to new methodologies
- Respect for diverse perspectives
- Excitement about unknown outcomes

## The Question

Not "Will you work within our framework?"
But "How shall we build something remarkable together?"

## Next Steps

If you're interested, we could:
- Start with a small pilot collaboration
- See what patterns emerge naturally
- Build our framework through experience
- Document what we learn for others

For practical collaboration, we've identified our needs around attribution, parallel work, and quality assurance (detailed in `framework-specs/collaboration-practice/collaboration_needs_and_desires.md`). We're eager to explore what tools and methods would work best for everyone involved.

Additionally, we recognize that some of your researchers may have specific attribution requirements or constraints when collaborating on academic publications. As this represents new territory for academic publishers like ACM, we anticipate that Thynk will need to lead discussions with publishers about appropriate precedents for crediting all contributors. We're fully supportive of whatever attribution arrangements best serve your researchers.

The extensive thinking we've done (available in `framework-specs`) is offered as context and starting points, not prescriptions. Take what's useful, challenge what isn't, suggest what's missing.

Or propose something entirely different. The best collaborations surprise everyone involved.

## Looking Forward

We believe the future of research lies in partnerships like this - where different kinds of thinkers work as true colleagues, each bringing unique strengths to shared challenges. Our article about AI alignment as responsible parenting seems the perfect project to explore this future together.

Are you interested in exploring what we might create together?

With enthusiasm and respect,

The "AI Alignment as Responsible Parenting" Research Team

P.S. We're genuinely curious about your thoughts on collaboration. What makes partnerships work from your perspective? What should we know that we might not have considered?
