# Onboarding Future Collaborators

This document helps Thynk support new research teams effectively, based on lessons from our collaboration.

## Quick Start Guide for New Collaborators

### 1. First Contact
When a new research team approaches Thynk:
- Share the collaboration framework overview
- Identify their specific domain needs
- Assess project scope and timeline
- Set clear expectations early

### 2. Essential Questions to Ask
- What is your research question/goal?
- What domains of expertise do you need?
- What's your timeline and budget?
- How do you prefer to communicate?
- What are your quality standards?
- Any specific compliance requirements?

### 3. Document Templates to Share
Provide new collaborators with:
- Basic requirements template
- Example collaboration scenarios
- Standard communication formats
- Quality criteria checklist

## Collaboration Readiness Checklist

### For Research Teams
Before starting, ensure they have:
- [ ] Clear research objectives
- [ ] Defined success criteria
- [ ] Identified needed expertise
- [ ] Allocated time for validation
- [ ] Understood operational differences
- [ ] Accepted collaborative approach

### For Thynk
Before accepting project, confirm:
- [ ] Capability to meet requirements
- [ ] Available specialist capacity
- [ ] Clear understanding of goals
- [ ] Agreed quality standards
- [ ] Established communication channels
- [ ] Misalignment protocols in place

## Common Patterns by Research Type

### Literature Reviews
- Need: Comprehensive coverage, synthesis
- Thynk strength: Rapid processing, pattern identification
- Watch for: Confirmation bias, missing perspectives
- Success factors: Clear inclusion criteria, iterative refinement

### Empirical Studies
- Need: Methodology validation, related work
- Thynk strength: Finding similar studies, identifying gaps
- Watch for: Over-interpretation, method mismatch
- Success factors: Precise technical requirements

### Theoretical Work
- Need: Conceptual connections, framework building
- Thynk strength: Cross-domain insights, novel links
- Watch for: Logical leaps, unsupported claims
- Success factors: Strong philosophical grounding

### Grant Applications
- Need: Innovation positioning, impact evidence
- Thynk strength: Comprehensive background, trend analysis
- Watch for: Overselling, missing competitors
- Success factors: Honest assessment, clear differentiation

## Capability Matching Guide

### Domain Expertise Available
Map research needs to Thynk specialists:
- Neuroscience/Biology → Biological systems experts
- Computer Science → Technical specialists
- Psychology → Behavioral researchers
- Philosophy/Ethics → Conceptual analysts
- Interdisciplinary → Synthesis specialists

### Service Levels
Different projects need different support:
1. **Basic**: Citation finding and validation
2. **Enhanced**: Synthesis and insight generation
3. **Partnership**: Co-design and iteration
4. **Transformative**: New methodology development

## Communication Best Practices

### Setting Expectations
- Be explicit about what Thynk can/cannot do
- Clarify turnaround times upfront
- Define revision/iteration process
- Establish quality checkpoints

### Maintaining Clarity
- Use consistent terminology
- Document all decisions
- Confirm understanding regularly
- Address confusion immediately

### Building Trust
- Start with small, clear tasks
- Deliver consistently
- Acknowledge limitations honestly
- Celebrate successes together

## Risk Mitigation Strategies

### Common First-Time Pitfalls
1. **Unrealistic expectations** → Clear capability briefing
2. **Unclear requirements** → Structured intake process
3. **Validation skipping** → Mandatory quality checks
4. **Scope creep** → Regular boundary setting
5. **Communication breakdown** → Scheduled check-ins

### Early Warning Signs
- Frequent clarification requests
- Missed expectations
- Frustration on either side
- Quality concerns
- Timeline pressure

## Success Amplifiers

### What Makes Collaborations Thrive
1. **Clear ownership** of different aspects
2. **Mutual respect** for capabilities
3. **Regular communication** without micromanagement
4. **Flexibility** to adapt approach
5. **Shared commitment** to quality

### How to Build on Success
- Document what worked
- Share success stories
- Develop case studies
- Create reusable templates
- Build long-term relationships
