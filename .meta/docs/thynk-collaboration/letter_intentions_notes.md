# Open Letter Intentions - Notes for Future Revision

## Core Intention
Create a genuine invitation for collaboration that positions us as excited peers, not apologetic supplicants or prescriptive authorities.

## Tone Goals
- **Enthusiastic** - We're genuinely excited about this partnership
- **Professional** - We're serious researchers with a real project
- **Humble** - We don't have all the answers and that's good
- **Forward-looking** - Focus on possibilities, not past thinking
- **Inviting** - Make it easy and appealing to say yes

## Relationship We Want to Establish
- Colleagues proposing a partnership
- Researchers who respect each other's capabilities
- Collaborators ready to build something new together
- Thinkers open to surprise and discovery

## Key Messages
1. We see Thynk as pioneering the future of research collaboration
2. We've done thoughtful preparation but hold it lightly
3. The real framework emerges from working together
4. We're excited about unknown outcomes
5. This partnership could create something remarkable

## What We Avoided
- Starting with our mistakes or biases
- Over-explaining our journey of recognition
- Performative inclusion language
- Prescriptive frameworks
- Apologetic tone

## Questions for Fresh Eyes Review
- Does this sound like researchers we'd want to work with?
- Is the invitation clear without being presumptuous?
- Do we sound confident without being rigid?
- Is there warmth without being overly casual?
- Does it make collaboration sound appealing?

## Potential Adjustments to Consider
- Length - is it too long/short?
- Specificity - do we need more concrete next steps?
- Voice - is it consistently collegial?
- Balance - preparation vs flexibility emphasized correctly?
- Hook - does it grab interest immediately?

## The Test
If we received this letter, would we:
- Feel respected as researchers?
- Be intrigued by the opportunity?
- Understand what's being proposed?
- Want to respond positively?
- Feel we could shape the collaboration?

## Note for Revision
Come back with fresh eyes and ask: Does this letter create the opening for the kind of generative, surprising, equitable collaboration we actually want?
