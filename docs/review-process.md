# Review Process for Citation Validation

This document outlines how we'll work with Thynk's Deep Research team to ensure the quality and appropriateness of citations for our ACM AI Alignment article.

## Two-Stage Review Process

### Stage 1: Initial Citation Discovery
1. Research team members find relevant citations for each claim
2. Multiple perspectives gathered (e.g., both neuroscience and AI safety views)
3. Initial relevance assessment provided

### Stage 2: Independent Review
1. Different team members review proposed citations
2. Fresh perspective without initial search bias
3. Critical evaluation of claim-citation fit

## Review Criteria

### Relevance Assessment
- Does the citation directly support the claim?
- Is the connection clear or does it require interpretation?
- Are there stronger alternatives available?

### Quality Evaluation
- Is the source peer-reviewed?
- What is the publication's impact factor or reputation?
- How recent is the research?
- Has it been replicated or challenged?

### Interdisciplinary Validity
- Does the citation translate well across domains?
- Are we making appropriate analogies?
- Do specialists from different fields agree on interpretation?

## Specific Review Needs

### High-Priority Reviews
These claims are central to our argument and need extra scrutiny:

1. **"Mesa-optimizers can spawn internal optimizers with misaligned goals"**
   - Need: AI safety specialist primary review
   - Second opinion: Systems theory or evolutionary biology perspective

2. **"Chronic stress fundamentally rewires neural pathways"**
   - Need: Neuroscience specialist primary review
   - Second opinion: How this translates to AI system pressure

3. **"Secure attachment predicts better emotional regulation"**
   - Need: Psychology specialist primary review
   - Second opinion: Applicability to trust relationships with AI systems

4. **"Moral responsibility requires both agency and understanding"**
   - Need: Philosophy specialist primary review
   - Second opinion: AI ethics and legal perspectives

### Cross-Domain Claims
These claims bridge multiple fields and need interdisciplinary review:

1. **Biological-AI parallels** (e.g., neural pruning → model compression)
   - Review by both domain specialists
   - Assessment of analogy validity

2. **Parenting-AI supervision parallels**
   - Child development specialist review
   - AI safety specialist review
   - Evaluation of metaphor limits

## Review Questions

### For Initial Research Team
1. Why did you select this citation?
2. What other options did you consider?
3. How confident are you in the match?
4. Any caveats or limitations?

### For Reviewers
1. Is this the best available citation for this claim?
2. Are there any misinterpretations or stretches?
3. Would you suggest alternatives?
4. How would you rate the citation strength (1-10)?
5. Any ethical or controversial considerations?

## Review Outcomes

### Possible Recommendations
- **Accept**: Citation strongly supports claim
- **Accept with caveats**: Good support but note limitations
- **Replace**: Better alternatives available
- **Supplement**: Need additional citations for full support
- **Reconsider**: Claim itself may need revision

### Documentation
For each reviewed citation:
- Original recommendation
- Reviewer assessment
- Final decision
- Justification for any changes

## Benefits of Review Process

1. **Quality Assurance**: Catch weak or inappropriate citations
2. **Interdisciplinary Validation**: Ensure cross-domain claims hold up
3. **Fresh Perspectives**: Overcome confirmation bias
4. **Stronger Arguments**: Build more robust support for claims
5. **Academic Rigor**: Meet high standards for publication

## Timeline Integration

- Week 1-2: Initial citation discovery
- Week 2-3: Review process runs in parallel with continued research
- Week 3: Incorporate review feedback
- Week 4: Final validation pass

This review process ensures our article maintains the highest academic standards while benefiting from diverse perspectives from Thynk's Deep Research team.
